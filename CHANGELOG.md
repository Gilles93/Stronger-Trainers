# Changelog

## 1.7.0

### Yellow and Blue support

Rival battles were broken in Yellow. You'd meet a lone level 6 starter at Oak's
lab, then again on Route 22, then again in Cerulean, each time a different
species. Yellow lays its rival parties out differently from Red and Blue (three
where Red has nine), so the shared roster table was landing on the wrong battles.

Rosters are now per version. Yellow's rival fights with the Eevee line, evolving
into Jolteon, Flareon or Vaporeon along whichever branch the game settled at the
lab, backed by Sandslash, Alakazam, Exeggutor and the rest.

Yellow's leaders were also being made *weaker* than the unmodded game. Vanilla
Yellow's Koga, Sabrina, Blaine and Giovanni all sit higher than the levels this
mod used to give them. Fixed.

### Boss levels are calculated

Previously they were vanilla plus a few levels, chosen by feel. That's why the
later gyms kept going soft: this mod raises every ordinary trainer, pads their
parties and evolves their pre-evolutions, so you bank far more experience than
vanilla hands out, and it compounds.

Levels now come from walking the game's route order, paying out every trainer at
the mod's own settings with the engine's experience formula, and inverting the
growth curve to find the level you'll actually be. Bosses sit above that by a
margin ramping from +2 at Brock to +14 at the Champion. Yellow is calculated
separately. Nothing ends up below what that version already fielded.

Mid-game rival fights moved with them: the S.S. Anne battle from 22 to 35, the
Pokémon Tower one from 28 to 45, Silph from 43 to 53.

### Type coverage on gym teams

Gym teams still keep their type absolutely, but they can now hurt what used to
wall them. Brock's Rhyhorn carries Thunderbolt and his Kabuto carries Ice Beam,
both legal TMs, and it's still six Rock Pokémon. Misty answers Electric with
Earthquake on Poliwhirl and Dig on Psyduck. Koga's Arbok answers Ground with an
Earthquake of its own.

Coverage is spread across the team rather than stacked on the ace. Every moveset
is still checked against the ROM's learnsets and TM lists.

Two dead slots removed while I was in there: Whirlwind, which fails outright
against a trainer in Gen 1, and Sky Attack, which spends a turn charging that the
AI doesn't account for.

### Bosses switch

Bosses now switch to whichever of their team the matchup favours. No Gen 1
trainer does this; the engine's own routine takes the first unfainted Pokémon
regardless of matchup.

It decides on species types, both sides, which is what you can see across the
field. Switching costs the boss its turn, so you get a free move each time.
Capped per fight, one turn of grace after every send-out so it can't ping-pong,
and it won't rotate away from a Pokémon that can already finish you or out of a
matchup it's winning.

New rows: `BOSS SWITCHING` (on) and `SWITCHES PER FIGHT` (2). Off is exact
vanilla behaviour.

### Padding brings variety

Short trainer parties used to be filled out with copies of what the trainer
already had. They now get a different Pokémon sharing one of his types: a
lone-Onix Hiker brings a Rhyhorn and a Geodude, a Sailor's Shellder brings a
Psyduck and a Seel.

Only species that appear in wild encounter tables are eligible, which keeps out
every fossil, game corner prize, gift Pokémon and legendary. Picks favour the
closest type match and skip anything found in the wild well above that trainer's
level. The same trainer always brings the same Pokémon.

New row: `PAD WITH VARIETY` (on).

### Also

- The Celadon Chief's roster could never apply outside Red, because Blue and
  Yellow ship no party data for that class and the mod bailed out before
  reaching its own roster.
- Boss rosters are generated by tooling that now lives in the repo, with a
  validator that rejects an unknown species, an illegal move, or a party index
  the target version doesn't actually have.
